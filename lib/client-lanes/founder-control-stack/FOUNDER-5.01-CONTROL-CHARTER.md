# Founder 5.01% Control Charter

Model drafting architecture for Delaware counsel review. This template separates economic
ownership, stockholder voting control, board architecture, class protections, and contractual
consent rights. It is not a certificate ready for filing and does not override mandatory DGCL,
fiduciary, securities, exchange, tax, accounting, or investor requirements.

## Article I — Capital Structure

The certificate should authorize three common classes:

- **Class A Investor Common:** one vote per share and the baseline economic rights.
- **Class B Founder Common:** identical per-share economics unless otherwise stated, with the
  enhanced voting ratio, permitted-transferee rules, separate Class B protections, and conversion
  mechanics stated in the certificate.
- **Class C Non-Voting Common:** economic participation with no general vote except as required
  by law or the certificate; intended uses include employee compensation and acquisitions.

The certificate must state authorized shares, class rights, preferences, limitations, conversion,
separate-class voting, director designation, and adverse-change protections with precision.

## Article II — Founder Threshold

“Founder Threshold” means beneficial ownership by the Founder and defined Permitted Founder
Entities of at least **5.01%** of the defined applicable equity denominator. The documents must
specify whether the test is outstanding, as-converted, or fully diluted; the measurement date;
attribution rules; cure period; record-date rules; and treatment of trusts, controlled entities,
voting agreements, and derivative securities.

## Article III — Class B Super-Voting Rights

The initial fixed-ratio design should be modeled at **100 votes per Class B share**, with Class A
at one vote per share. At 5.01% founder economics and 94.99% one-vote non-founder economics:

```text
Founder votes = 5.01 × 100 = 501
Other votes   = 94.99 × 1   = 94.99
Total votes   = 595.99
Founder power = 501 / 595.99 ≈ 84.1%
```

The model must also show 10:1, 20:1, 25:1, 50:1, and 100:1. For founder economic percentage
`p` and multiplier `M`, the simplified voting-power formula is:

```text
Founder voting power = pM / (pM + 100 - p)
```

For `p = 5.01` and a target above `50.1%`, `M` must exceed approximately `19.03`. Therefore,
20:1 is only a boundary case. It is not a sufficient safety margin for future issuances,
conversions, or other voting securities.

Any variable voting formula is an alternative requiring separate Delaware counsel review under
DGCL §§151, 212, and 216. It must not be treated as a default or assumed enforceable.

## Article IV — Control-Preservation Mechanism

While the Founder Threshold is satisfied, the certificate and related documents should preserve
the agreed Founder Voting Percentage, subject to applicable law. The control package must keep
separate: Class B vote power; Class B protective votes; director designation; board authority and
fiduciary duties; and §122(18) contractual consent rights.

## Article V — Founder Director Election Rights

The certificate may provide Class B holders specified director election or designation rights.
For a seven-member initial board, define the number of Founder designees, replacement rights,
vacancies, removal, board-size protections, and any class-vote mechanics. Committee membership
rights remain subject to independence, exchange, fiduciary, and applicable-law constraints.

## Article VI — Founder Reserved Matters

The reserved-matters schedule should cover, at minimum:

1. capital structure, Class B rights, recapitalization, reclassification, and conversion;
2. issuances of senior, super-voting, preferred, convertible, or otherwise adverse securities;
3. mergers, acquisitions, sale of substantially all assets, reorganizations, spin-offs,
   domestication, dissolution, change of control, and material subsidiaries;
4. debt, liens, guarantees, convertible debt, material refinancing, and IP pledges;
5. board size, Founder designees, committee protections, Founder Rights Agreement amendments,
   waivers, termination, and inconsistent governance documents.

Each item must identify Board approval, committee approval, generally applicable stockholder vote,
separate Class B vote, Founder Consent, threshold, notice, response period, evidence, emergency
exception, and direct/indirect aggregation rule.

## Article VII — Class B Protective Vote

No amendment that disproportionately and adversely eliminates, reduces, modifies, circumvents,
or materially impairs Class B rights should become effective without all legally required
approvals and any separate Class B approval validly established in the certificate.

## Article VIII — Anti-Circumvention

Covered actions should be tested by substance and aggregated across subsidiaries, affiliates,
merger subsidiaries, convertible issuances, transfers followed by dispositions, recapitalizations,
and related transactions. The agreement must state the scope without purporting to override
mandatory corporate approvals or fiduciary duties.

## Article IX — Permitted Founder Transfers

Define permitted transfers to Founder-controlled entities, revocable or qualifying irrevocable
trusts, estate-planning vehicles, family trusts, foundations, and the Founder’s estate. Preserve
Class B only while the stated beneficial-ownership and control conditions remain satisfied.

## Article X — Automatic Conversion

Except for permitted transfers, transferred Class B shares should automatically convert to Class A
at the stated ratio. The certificate must address notice, record date, partial transfers, nominee
or beneficial ownership, involuntary transfers, creditor attachment, divorce, and administrative
implementation through the transfer ledger.

## Article XI — 5.01% Sunset

On definitive failure of the Founder Threshold, after the stated measurement and cure procedures,
the documents should specify whether Class B converts, voting power resets, director designation
steps down, Reserved Matters terminate or tier down, and which information or registration rights
survive. Do not leave continuous versus record-date testing implicit.

## Article XII — Ownership Calculation

Define the denominator and treatment of issued/outstanding shares, treasury shares, options,
warrants, RSUs, SAFEs, notes, convertible preferred, earn-outs, contingent shares, splits,
reverse splits, recapitalizations, reorganizations, subsidiaries, Founder entities, trusts,
voting agreements, beneficial ownership, and derivatives. Maintain both legal outstanding and
fully diluted schedules where they serve different purposes.

## Article XIII — Founder Participation Right

The Founder Rights Agreement may provide preemptive, participation, top-up, or equivalent rights
to give the Founder an opportunity to remain at or above 5.01%. The model must show the purchase
needed when a financing would otherwise move Founder ownership from, for example, 5.20% to 4.60%.
Exceptions for employee plans, acquisitions, strategic issuances, emergency financing, and
investor commitments must be explicit and reviewed for securities, fiduciary, solvency, and
equal-treatment issues.

## Article XIV — Board and Committees

Audit, compensation, nominating/governance, finance, M&A, executive, and risk committees may be
established. Founder representation remains subject to independence and fiduciary requirements;
committee membership must not be treated as a substitute for the separate Founder Consent lane.

## Article XV — Founder Rights Agreement

The Corporation and Founder may enter a separate Founder Rights Agreement under applicable
Delaware law, including §122(18), for specified contractual consent, information, anti-
circumvention, amendment, waiver, and termination rights. The agreement must distinguish Founder
as stockholder from Founder as director or officer and must identify board-approved consideration,
contracting parties, notice, evidence, response, and enforcement mechanics.

## Article XVI — Control Stack and Document Allocation

| Layer | Instrument | Primary protection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Certificate | classes, votes, conversion, separate votes, director designation |
| 2 | Certificate Class B protections | adverse-change and Class B separate approval |
| 3 | Stockholder voting | enhanced voting power while threshold is satisfied |
| 4 | Board architecture | Founder designation and board-size protection |
| 5 | Founder Rights Agreement | §122(18) Reserved Matters and consent |
| 6 | Participation protection | opportunity to maintain 5.01% threshold |
| 7 | Anti-circumvention | direct/indirect and series-transaction coverage |
| 8 | Succession | permitted trusts, estate vehicles, and conversion rules |

Bylaws and resolutions carry mechanics; the cap table, vote ledger, transfer ledger, beneficial-
ownership register, and accounting workpapers prove operation. The control stack must not imply
that a Founder can exercise powers reserved to the Board, a committee, stockholders, or another
corporate body by mandatory law.

## Article XVII — Fundamental Principle

The design separates economic ownership, voting control, and contractual consent. Satisfaction of
the Founder Threshold does not grant unilateral authority over mandatory corporate functions.
Every implementation must clear Delaware corporate, securities, investor, tax, accounting,
valuation, estate/trust, fiduciary, exchange, and adversarial review.

estate/trust, and local-counsel review; not legal advice and not a filing-ready instrument.
