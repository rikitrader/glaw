## Topic Index Coverage Snapshot

The Schwab topic index was paginated into four pages as of 2026-08-13. GLAW's trust intake
should assume the public education market treats these as distinct client questions:

- Estate-tax uncertainty: A-B/bypass, GRAT, CRT, state estate tax, exemption flexibility.
- SLATs: separate-property funding, indirect spousal access, community-property partition,
  reciprocal-trust doctrine, GST/dynasty optionality, basis tradeoff, death/divorce access risk.
- Dynasty trusts: GST allocation, perpetuity/duration law, situs, trustee location, investment
  tax management, creditor/divorce protection, HEMS or incentive distribution standards.
- ILITs: IRA-distribution funding economics, RMD and ordinary-income modeling, underwriting,
  policy design, Form 709, premium gifts, Crummey notices, section 2035 for existing policies.
- QPRTs: retained term, section 7520 valuation, property-tax/homestead issues, term-survival
  risk, post-term leaseback, basis tradeoff, real-estate title and insurance review.
- Discretionary and particular-purpose trusts: distribution discretion, trustee discretion,
  special needs, divorce protection, beneficiary conflict, and fiduciary communication.
- Charitable split-interest trusts: CRT/CRAT/CRUT/NIMCRUT/CLT comparison, payout tests,
  charitable remainder or lead reporting, gain deferral, income stream, philanthropic intent.
- Administration basics: trust funding/title, beneficiary designations, trustee/executor choice,
  recordkeeping, incapacity planning, probate avoidance, family governance, and periodic review.

## Benchmark Themes to Preserve in GLAW

1. Trusts manage, distribute, and protect assets during life, incapacity, and after death.
2. Revocable trusts are probate/privacy/control tools; they must be funded to work.
3. Beneficiary designations and asset titling can override or bypass will/trust language.
4. Irrevocable trusts can support estate-tax, gift-tax, GST, creditor, and insurance planning,
   but they require professional drafting and administration.
5. A-B/bypass trust planning can still matter for state estate tax, GST preservation, and
   older documents with mandatory split language.
6. GRAT, CRT, dynasty trust, SLAT, and ILIT planning each has different tax, access, basis,
   trustee, and administration risks.
7. Trust situs matters: state income tax, asset-protection law, dynasty/perpetuities rules,
   trustee location, and corporate trustee availability can change outcomes.
8. Discretionary trusts can reduce brittle distribution rules, but trustee selection and
   fiduciary governance become more important.
9. Estate plans need family/governance communication: equal vs equitable distributions,
   special-needs heirs, stepchildren/ex-spouses/indirect heirs, charitable intent, and
   personal-property memoranda.
10. Trust education materials are not individualized tax/legal advice; GLAW must keep the same
    caution and route final work to licensed tax, legal, trustee, insurance, and investment
    professionals.
11. SLAT workflow must verify separate-property funding, Form 709 reporting, community-property
    partition issues, basis tradeoffs, divorce/death access risk, beneficiary-spouse trustee
    limits, reciprocal-trust doctrine, and optional dynasty/GST design.
12. Dynasty trust workflow must include situs selection, GST allocation, trustee powers,
    perpetuities/duration law, beneficiary divorce/creditor design, income-tax posture, and
    family governance.
13. ILIT workflow must not treat IRA assets as directly transferable trust funding. Review RMD
    taxation, no basis step-up for inherited traditional IRAs, premium-gift mechanics, Form 709,
    Crummey notices, policy economics, incidents of ownership, and the section 2035 three-year
    rule when an existing policy is transferred.
14. Trustee readiness is a deliverable, not a footnote: name fiduciary capacity, investment
    authority, tax-return responsibility, recordkeeping duty, beneficiary communication cadence,
    and professional co-trustee/escalation options.
