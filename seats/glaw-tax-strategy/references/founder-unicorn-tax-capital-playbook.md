# Founder Unicorn Tax + Capital Playbook

This is the integrated GLAW playbook for a founder or sponsor trying to build, fund,
protect, and eventually exit a high-growth company or investment platform. It combines
tax strategy, capital raising, investor disclosure, QSBS substantiation, trust/LLC
sheltering, irrevocable-trust administration, credits, and adversarial review.

Use this when the matter mentions founder stock, QSBS, section 1202, section 83(b),
Form 15620, R&D credits, investment tax credits, raising money, Reg D, Reg CF, PPM,
investor suitability, dynasty trust planning, investment LLCs, or "unicorn" founder
planning.

## Core Principle

The goal is not to hide assets or sell tax magic. The goal is to own appreciating
assets in the right taxpayer, in the right entity, with the right securities exemption,
with contemporaneous records, and with enough business substance that an IRS agent,
SEC staff attorney, plaintiff, creditor, auditor, and investor can all understand the
structure.

No part of this playbook overrides `guardrails-and-substance.md`.

## Operating Model

Default topology for a founder/investor wealth plan:

```text
Founder / family
  |
  |-- Direct founder shares where QSBS, 83(b), issuer transfer limits, or grant mechanics require
  |
  |-- Irrevocable dynasty / investment trust
        |
        |-- Investment LLC for diversified assets, alternatives, marketable securities,
        |   real estate interests, private fund interests, and admin consolidation
        |
        |-- Direct trust-owned assets when tax or issuer rules make direct ownership preferable
```

Default topology for the operating company/capital raise:

```text
Founder(s) / permitted trusts / investor vehicles
  |
  |-- Delaware C-corp operating company if QSBS/VC path controls
        |
        |-- Option plan / founder restricted stock / IP assignment
        |-- Investor financing: SAFE, preferred stock, Reg D, Reg CF, or other exemption
        |-- QSBS issuer substantiation packet and shareholder information statement
```

## QSBS Issuer Substantiation Packet

Every QSBS-sensitive company must maintain an issuer-side file because shareholders usually
cannot prove eligibility without company records. This mirrors the policy goal of the proposed
revenue procedure to standardize information corporations give shareholders to show that stock
is QSBS.

Minimum packet:

- Issuer legal name, EIN, jurisdiction, entity history, predecessor history, and C-corp status dates.
- Stock class, certificate or book-entry ID, issuance date, recipient, consideration, and whether
  acquired directly from issuer or through an underwriter.
- Gross-asset test ledger immediately before and after each issuance, including cash raised and
  contributed-property basis rules.
- Active-business file showing at least 80% asset use in qualified business during substantially
  all of the shareholder's holding period.
- Excluded-business screen: services, finance, brokerage, investment management, farming,
  mining/depletion, hotel/restaurant, and other statutory exclusions.
- Redemption hygiene log for redemptions from the holder/related persons and significant
  corporate redemptions during the statutory lookback windows.
- Section 83(b)/Form 15620 packet for restricted founder stock, including proof of timely filing
  and company copy.
- Transfer history, gifts, trust transfers, conversions, exchanges, recapitalizations, and any
  section 1045 rollover data.
- Board minutes and capitalization records supporting every issuance, financing, redemption,
  conversion, and acquisition.
- Shareholder information statement for each relevant holder at financing, annual refresh, and
  exit/secondary event.

## Shareholder Information Statement

The company should give each holder a standardized statement whenever stock is issued,
transferred, sold, repurchased, or when a financing/exit makes QSBS relevant.

Include:

- Holder name and taxpayer classification as known to issuer.
- Issuer name, EIN, jurisdiction, and C-corp status confirmation.
- Stock class, number of shares, issue date, issue price, consideration, and original-issuance
  representation.
- Whether the issuer believes the gross-asset test was met at issuance, with the tested amount
  or "not determined" if unavailable.
- Active-business representation for the relevant period or a precise limitation if not yet known.
- Redemption/repurchase disclosure for the relevant statutory windows.
- Transfer and conversion history known to the issuer.
- Warning that eligibility is determined at shareholder level and requires holder-specific tax
  review, especially for trusts, partnerships, LLCs, married taxpayers, gifts, inheritance,
  hedging, offsetting positions, and section 1045.
- Contact, record date, and version number.

## Direct Trust Ownership Carve-Out Matrix

The investment LLC is the default administrative wrapper for diversified investments, but not
the universal answer.

| Asset | Default holder | Reason | Required review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketable securities / ETFs / diversified alternatives | Trust-owned investment LLC | Consolidated management, charging-order layer, clean administration. | Investment policy, fiduciary powers, state income tax, margin/custody terms |
| Founder restricted stock / section 83(b) shares | Direct founder or directly by permitted trust only if counsel structures issuance that way before transfer | Timing, original issuance, service-recipient, vesting, and 30-day election mechanics are fragile. | `glaw-83b-election`, corporate counsel, tax counsel |
| QSBS C-corp shares | Often direct non-corporate holder or direct trust holder; avoid automatic LLC interposition | Section 1202 is shareholder-specific and depends on original issuance, non-corporate holder, holding period, active business, and transfers. | Tax strategy, PE/VC counsel, issuer transfer consents, QSBS packet |
| S-corp stock | Direct eligible trust only | S-corp eligibility can be destroyed by the wrong shareholder. | QSST/ESBT/grantor trust analysis and timely elections |
| Life insurance | ILIT direct owner | Estate-tax and policy ownership rules drive the structure. | ILIT counsel, Crummey notices, premium-gift calendar |
| Retirement assets | Usually not trust/LLC-owned during life | Retirement tax rules and prohibited-transaction rules control; beneficiary designations usually matter more. | ERISA/qualified-plan/IRA review |
| Real estate or operating-risk assets | LLC/SPE under trust or holdco | Liability isolation and lender/SPE requirements. | Real-estate counsel, lender consent, insurance |
| Private fund / SPV interests | Trust or investment LLC depending on subscription docs | Transfer, KYC, tax forms, ERISA, and securities restrictions vary. | PE/VC counsel, subscription documents, AML/OFAC |
| Crypto/digital assets | Trust-owned LLC or qualified custody depending on risk | Custody, authority, fiduciary control, tax lot tracking. | Custody policy, wallet control, tax reporting |

## Irrevocable Trust Administration

As opposed to revocable trusts, GRATs, QPRTs, irrevocable gift trusts, and ILITs are
irrevocable: the grantor generally cannot change them after execution except through
limited powers expressly allowed by the instrument and applicable law. Many are drafted
as grantor trusts for income-tax purposes during the grantor's lifetime, so the grantor
pays income tax on trust income while appreciation can be moved outside the taxable
estate if the structure is respected.

Trustee administration focus:

| Trust type | Primary purpose | Trustee focus |
|---|---|---|
| GRAT | Transfer appreciation above the section 7520 hurdle. | Annuity payments, valuation support, term calendar, no prohibited commutation/exchange unless counsel approves. |
| QPRT | Transfer residence at a discounted value while grantor retains term use. | Residence use, expenses, insurance, term-end transfer/leaseback, no unauthorized sale/refinance. |
| Irrevocable gift trust / dynasty trust | Completed gift, GST planning, creditor/divorce protection for descendants. | Separate accounts, discretionary distributions, GST allocation, Crummey notices if used, fiduciary investment process. |
| IDGT | Estate freeze with grantor-trust income-tax treatment. | Installment note payments, seed capital, valuation, grantor-tax burn, no sloppy commingling. |
| ILIT | Keep life insurance proceeds outside estate. | Policy ownership, premium gifts, Crummey notices, beneficiary records, premium calendar, incidents-of-ownership guardrails. |

## How to Raise Money Without Breaking the Tax Plan

1. Pick the exemption before soliciting: Reg D 506(b), Reg D 506(c), Reg CF, Reg A,
   Reg S, intrastate, or registered offering.
2. Match the investor base to the exemption: accredited investors, qualified purchasers,
   qualified clients, retail crowdfunding investors, non-U.S. investors, tax-exempt/foreign
   investors needing blockers, or strategic investors.
3. Build the disclosure stack: PPM/OM or Form C, investor presentation, risk factors,
   use-of-proceeds, capitalization, dilution, related-party transactions, conflicts,
   management biographies, financial statements, material contracts, transfer restrictions,
   tax discussion, and no-reliance/suitability acknowledgments.
4. Keep QSBS marketing precise: state that QSBS is conditional, must be verified at the holder
   level, may change with future facts, and is not guaranteed.
5. Gate compensation: placement agents, finders, referral fees, transaction-based compensation,
   and cap-intro arrangements require broker-dealer analysis.
6. Docket filings: Form D, state blue sky, Reg CF/Form C amendments/annual reports, investor
   notices, BOI updates, franchise tax, annual reports, 83(b), and tax return attachments.

## Investor-Purpose Deliverables

For every investor-facing raise, produce:

- Investor thesis memo: why the structure exists, how money flows, what investors own, and
  what the exit case is.
- Securities exemption memo: exemption, solicitation limits, investor eligibility, filing
  calendar, integration risk, bad-actor check, broker-dealer/finder analysis.
- Tax attributes memo: QSBS posture, passthrough vs C-corp treatment, credit/incentive posture,
  1045 rollover possibilities, state tax, withholding, K-1/1099/shareholder statement.
- Risk-factor bank: tax, securities, dilution, liquidity, valuation, management conflicts,
  related-party, regulatory, transfer restriction, leverage, custody, and reporting risk.
- Investor questionnaire and subscription agreement.
- Data-room index: charter, bylaws/OA, consents, cap table, financials, tax-credit support,
  IP assignment, material contracts, litigation/regulatory disclosures, QSBS packet.

## Credits and Incentives Screen

Screen each company for:

- R&D credit and payroll-tax offset.
- R&E capitalization/expensing treatment and book-tax differences.
- Section 195 startup costs and section 248 organizational costs.
- State investment, research, jobs, training, brownfields, historic, housing, climatetech,
  qualified conversion, internship, energy, and industry credits.
- Transferability/refundability, recapture, certification, wage/job/capex commitments, and
  owner/member attribution rules.

No credit goes into investor materials until `glaw-credit-strategy` builds a substantiation
checklist and `glaw-adversarial` attacks it as an IRS examiner.
