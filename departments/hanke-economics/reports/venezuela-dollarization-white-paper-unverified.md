# PART I — Executive Policy Assessment

## 1. Executive Summary

Venezuela’s dollarization question is a question about institutional redesign, not simply currency substitution. Formal dollarization would affect the central bank, commercial banks, government financing, public contracts, wages, pensions, debt service, payment systems, and external adjustment.

The principal possible benefit is monetary credibility. The principal possible cost is the loss of domestic monetary flexibility and a conventional lender of last resort. The outcome would depend on reserve liquidity, bank capitalization, fiscal adjustment, external dollar inflows, debt restructuring, legal execution, and institutional credibility.

**Current evidence status:** current BCV monetary aggregates, SUDEBAN banking aggregates, liquid reserves, fiscal financing, net oil FX, current PDVSA debt, and USD circulation are not sufficiently verified for a decision-grade conclusion.

# PART III — Monetary History and Dollarization Models

## 7. Bolívar Regimes and Credibility

The analysis must distinguish redenominations, official exchange-rate regimes, parallel-market behavior, monetary financing, inflation expectations, and currency substitution. Historical monetary evidence is not automatically current evidence.

## 8. Dollarization Definitions

The report compares informal dollar use, partial dollarization, currency boards, full official dollarization, synthetic dollar balances, stablecoin layers, and currency competition. These systems have different reserve, legal, banking, and fiscal requirements.

# PART IV — Balance-Sheet Requirement

## 9. Dollar Conversion Requirement

The required conversion amount depends on the monetary object being converted:

```text
Physical currency = VES currency in circulation / R
Monetary base     = VES monetary base / R
M1                = VES M1 / R
M2                = VES M2 / R
```

The report must not equate M2 with physical dollars. Bank deposits, reserves, credit, foreign assets, and cash outside banks must be modeled separately.

## 10. Reserves

Gross reserves, liquid reserves, gold, SDRs, foreign securities, encumbered assets, and immediately usable FX must be separated. Gold is not automatically equivalent to deployable dollar liquidity.

# PART V — Banking System

## 11. Commercial-Bank Balance Sheets

Required variables are deposits, loans, reserves, securities, capital, NPLs, provisions, government exposure, USD assets, USD liabilities, and maturity structure. No bank-specific solvency conclusion may be issued without reliable bank-level evidence.

## 12. Credit-Contraction Risk

Possible sequence:

```text
Dollarization → reserve scarcity → tighter lending → lower investment
→ employment pressure → lower demand → weaker fiscal revenue
```

This is a `MODELED` mechanism, not a forecast. The credit-to-GDP elasticity must be estimated from verified data.

# PART VI — Lender of Last Resort

## 13. Liquidity Backstop

Dollarization would constrain the central bank’s ability to create domestic base money. Alternatives include a fiscal liquidity fund, external credit lines, multilateral facilities, private interbank liquidity, deposit insurance, and an oil-backed stabilization facility.

Required stress cases are 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50% deposit withdrawals.

# PART VII — Fiscal Policy

## 14. Fiscal Sustainability

The fiscal model must separate oil revenue, taxes, royalties, customs, wages, pensions, subsidies, health, education, security, infrastructure, interest, and transfers.

```text
Fiscal balance = revenue − expenditure
Primary balance = revenue − non-interest expenditure
Interest/revenue = interest expense / government revenue
```

The loss of monetary financing would require fiscal replacement. The size of that adjustment is currently `UNAVAILABLE`.

## 15. Seigniorage

Historical seigniorage must be estimated from verified monetary and price data. It must not be inferred from a narrative description alone.

# PART VIII — Oil and External Accounts

## 16. Net Oil FX

Gross oil revenue is not sovereign liquidity. The final model must subtract operating costs, imports, partner shares, royalties, taxes, domestic consumption, debt service, and blocked or encumbered funds.

```text
Net sovereign oil FX = gross oil revenue − committed dollar outflows
```

## 17. Balance of Payments

Oil exports, non-oil exports, remittances, FDI, borrowing, imports, debt service, capital flight, and profit repatriation must be reconciled in a dollar-flow waterfall.

# PART IX — Debt and Legal Claims

## 18. Republic, PDVSA, and CITGO Exposure

The legal evidence stack confirms that creditor claims, awards, judgments, bond references, collateral, and enforcement proceedings exist, but these are not automatically a single debt total.

The CITGO stack records the PDVSA → PDVH → CITGO Holding → CITGO Petroleum chain, PDVH attachment litigation, collateral references, and enforcement evidence. The Crystallex judgment and OI appellate record are locally hashed and page-anchored.

## 19. Case-Loss Universe

The 80-case audit is active. A case becomes `VERIFIED` only after reading the award, judgment, indictment, docket, memorandum, exhibit, or official case record. Awards, settlements, judgments, interest, costs, forfeitures, and enforcement claims remain separate to prevent double counting.

# PART X — Households and Social Effects

## 20. Wages, Pensions, and Poverty

The final model will test wage and pension scenarios in USD and compare them with verified GDP, fiscal revenue, oil revenue, and household-income data. Current distributional winners and losers cannot be quantified responsibly.

Potentially exposed groups include bolívar-income households, pensioners, public employees, SMEs dependent on credit, and households without bank access.

# PART XI — Business and Investment

## 21. Private Sector

Dollarization could reduce exchange-rate risk for importers and exporters but could also increase working-capital constraints if banks cannot provide dollar credit. SMEs, construction, manufacturing, agriculture, retail, and oil-service firms require separate treatment.

# PART XII — Inflation and Real Exchange Rate

## 22. Inflation

Dollarization could suppress domestic monetary inflation but would not eliminate imported inflation, supply shocks, transportation costs, food-price changes, housing costs, or fiscal pressures.

```text
Inflation = monetary + exchange-rate + imported + supply + fiscal + structural components
```

The decomposition is not currently estimated because the required current series are incomplete.

# PART XIII — International Comparables

## 23. Ecuador, Panama, El Salvador, Zimbabwe, Argentina, Montenegro, and Kosovo

Comparables must be matched by banking depth, reserves, fiscal structure, debt, external income, legal institutions, and political conditions. Before-and-after comparisons alone do not establish causality.

# PART XIV — Economic Postures

## 24. Competing Interpretations

- **Hanke/currency-board:** credibility, hard constraints, convertibility, and reduced discretion.
- **Monetarist:** control of money growth, expectations, and inflation.
- **Keynesian:** adjustment output loss, unemployment, fiscal multipliers, and liquidity.
- **New Keynesian:** expectations, nominal rigidities, credibility, and transmission.
- **Austrian:** credit distortion, capital structure, and malinvestment.
- **IMF-style:** reserves, fiscal sustainability, banking solvency, debt, and balance of payments.
- **Central-bank:** payment settlement, emergency liquidity, and financial stability.

No posture is allowed to dominate automatically.

# PART XV — Red Team and Blue Team

## 25. Red-Team Questions

The Red Team must test whether dollarization fails through reserve scarcity, bank runs, fiscal insolvency, debt-service pressure, oil shocks, capital flight, legal barriers, sanctions, or political resistance.

## 26. Blue-Team Response
