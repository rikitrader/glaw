            Summary by the Governor of the Central Bank of BiH



T  he Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina is a state institution with the responsibili-
   ty for the independent implementation of monetary policy. Monetary policy in BH is
conducted by way of a Currency Board arrangement. Since the CBBH commenced its
operations in 1997, the BH currency, the convertible mark (KM), has been tied to the
Deutschemark at an exchange rate of one to one. As from 1 January, 2002, the KM is tied
to the Euro at an Exchange rate of 1 Euro equals 1.955830 KM. This is the same
exchange rate at which the DEM was tied to the Euro. This was done in order to ensure
that the citizens of BH and potential foreign investors in BH could continue to have
absolute confidence in the stability of the KM against its anchor currency.

During 2001, The Currency Board requirements of full convertibility and full foreign
exchange coverage for the CBBH's KM liabilities were met at all times. The CBBH balance
sheet continued to gain in strength during 2001 as the 'free foreign exchange reserves'
(i.e.) those that are above the level needed to cover all the KM liabilities) increased from
KM 48 million at the beginning of the year to KM 75 million at the end of 2001. As a
consequence of the soundness of the Currency Board, confidence in the stability of the
KM remained high during 2001 and inflation in BH remained low.

The use of the KM continued to grow steadily throughout the first nine months of the
year and then increased very rapidly in the last quarter when the DEM conversion flows
reached their peak. In the last three months of 2001, KM in circulation rose by KM 920
million, or 104 %. Coincidentally, citizens deposits with commercial banks also grew by
104%. This is a clear sign that citizens have started to trust the banking system with their
savings.

The large amounts of DEM converted in BH and the large proportion of DEM that were
converted either to KM currency or put on deposit in a BH bank led to an unprecedent-
ed growth in the country's official foreign exchange reserves. The CBBH's foreign assets
grew from KM 1,021 million at 31 December, 2000 to KM 2,697 million at 31 decem-
ber,2001, a growth of 164%. At the end of 2001, the CBBH's foreign reserves repre-
sented about five months of import coverage.

Another major task of the CBBH in 2001 was the introduction of the new domestic non-
cash payments system. The preparations for the start of the new system were carried out
in 2000 and involved cooperation from a large number of institutions. On 5 January, 2001
the payments bureaus ceased their functions in the payments system and non-cash pay-
ments started being carried out in commercial banks and cleared through new clearing
houses built and operated by the CBBH.

The new payment system has gone very smoothly during its first year of operation. There
have been few technical problems and it has been operational 99,9% of the time. There
have been no occasions on which settlement did not occur.

During 2001, number of transactions completed in the CBBH clearing houses was
11,916,222 worth KM 16,845 million.

In addition where both parties to a payment bank in the same commercial bank those
transactions are cleared and settled within that banks own accounting system.

The reform of the payment system has given Bosnia and Herzegovina a modern non-cash
payment system that is similar to the payment systems in most of Europe. However, the
payment system in BiH is still heavily dominated by cash payments and payment orders.
Alternative forms of payments, such as credit cards and e-banking are beginning to
appear and their significance in the Bosnia and Herzegovina payment system will
increase and, probably, increase rapidly. The Central Bank of BH will provide support to
these new forms of payment by making it possible for such transactions to be cleared and
settled through the RTGS system.

The banking sector in BiH continued to improve rapidly during 2001. The reform in the
banking sector has gone further and faster than reform in any other sector of the econo-
my. Competition is real, helped by the arrival of some good foreign banks.
Proportionately, foreign investment in the banking sector has been higher than in any
other sector in BiH.

Confidence is coming back and depositors are beginning to return to banks. Banks are
beginning to do more lending and to lend on more reasonable terms. However, interest
rates are still relatively high in BiH because the level of risk involved in bank lending is
still relatively high. The main factors causing this are the credit culture (too many loans
are not repaid), uncertain property rights, an incomplete legal framework, and a lack of
confidence in the court system with regard to commercial cases. Further improvements
will need to occur in all these areas before BiH interest rates will move to western euro-
pean levels.

During 2001. the Presidency of BiH approved the design of a KM 200 banknote. The sig-
nificant element of this decision was that the Presidency agreed that there should be only
one version of this bank note. The banknote will be put in circulation latter in 2002.

During 2001, the CBBH operated through its Head office in Sarajevo and five decentral-
ized offices (three main units and 2 branches). The number of employees increased by
51, largely because of an increase in the number of banking customers of the CBBH, a
decision to increase security within our offices, and the CBBH's new role in domestic pay-
ments system. By the end of 2001, the CBBH had 241 employees.

                                                                                                Introduction
                                     INTRODUCTION


T  he Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina was established in accordance with the
   Law adopted at the Parliament of Bosnia and Herzegovina on 20 June 1997. The
Central Bank started its operation on 11 August 1997. The main objectives and basic
tasks of the Central Bank, defined by the Law and in accordance with the General Peace
Agreement in Bosnia and Herzegovina are:

    - to achieve and maintain the stability of domestic currency (Convertible Mark) by
      issuing it according to the arrangement known as a Currency Board, i.e. with full
      coverage in freely convertible foreign exchange funds, under the fixed exchange
      rate: one Convertible Mark per one Deutsche Mark; Since 1 January 2002, KM is
      linked to Euro under the exchange rate 1 EUR = 1.955830 KM, i.e.
      1 KM= 0.511292 EUR, which was proposed in amendments for modification and
      supplements of the CBBH Law;
    - to define and control the implementation of monetary policy of Bosnia and
      Herzegovina;
    - to hold and manage, in a safe and profitable way, the official foreign exchange
      reserves;
    - to support or to establish and maintain appropriate payment and settlement
      systems;
    - to co-ordinate the activities of the Entity Banking Agencies which are in charge of
      bank licensing and supervision;
    - to receive deposits from institutions at the level of Bosnia and Herzegovina,
      deposits from Entities and their public institutions according to the joint decision of
      Entities, as well as deposits from commercial banks;
    - to issue rules and guidelines for the implementation of the activities of the Central
      Bank, within its powers as defined by the Law;
    - to take part in international organizations engaged in strengthening financial and
      economic stability, and to represent Bosnia and Herzegovina in intergovernmental
      organizations regarding issues of monetary policy.

The senior body of the Central Bank is the Governing Board, which is in charge of estab-
lishing and supervision of monetary policy, organization and strategies of the Central
Bank, all according to powers given to the Board by the Law. The Governing Board con-
sists of the Governor, who is Chairman of the Governing Board, and three members (two
from the Federation of BiH - Bosniak and Croat and one from Republika Srpska - Serb).
The Governor is a foreign national, Peter Nicholl. The Governor is also the chief execu-
tive in charge of daily operation of the CBBH. There is also the executive Management
of the Central Bank consisting of the Governor and three Vice Governors (appointed by
the Governor, with approval of the Governing Board). The Management is responsible
for the operational management of the CBBH.

The Central Bank operates through its Head Office and three Main Units in Sarajevo,
Mostar and Banja Luka (Main Unit Banja Luka also has a Branch at Pale) and Branch of the
Central Bank in Brcko District.

This report gives the review of activities and results of the CBBH work in 2001.

Monetary
 Policy                    I IMPLEMENTATION OF MONETARY POLICY



           D    uring 2001, monetary policy, as defined in the Law and Strategic Plan of the Central
                Bank, continued to be successfully implemented and improved. Main results of the
           policy are the following:

              - strict control of Currency Board principles;
              - continuous maintenance of the stability of domestic currency-Convertible Mark;
              - strict implementation of the Currency Board arrangement, by issuing domestic
                currency, exclusively with full coverage in freely convertible exchange funds;
              - The official foreign exchange reserves held with the Central Bank are managed
                according to the Governing Board guidelines, so that their investment is carried out
                taking care about the minimization of the risk and achieving of the higher profit. In
                the end of 2000, foreign exchange reserves amounted to KM 1,021 million, in the
                end of September 2001, foreign exchange reserves were around KM 1,235 million
                and in the end of December 2001 they amounted to KM 2,697 million. This means
                that in the last quartile of the last year they increased by KM 1,462 million or 118%
                (which is the result of conversion of DEM and other currencies of Euro area to Euro,
                and to KM also), and they increased by 164% compared to the year 2000;
              - Monetary liabilities represent the liability of the Central Bank for the KM banknotes
                and coins in circulation and the reserve deposits of the resident banks at the CBBH;
                Net free reserves, representing the surplus of coverage of monetary liabilities
                amount to KM 75 million;
              - Foreign assets are composed mostly of the deposits with the foreign banks, as it is
                prescribed by the Article 35. of the Law, the balance sheet also shows the
                composition of the holding of the currencies differentiating them in EUR and other
                currencies;
              - provision of the full presence of the KM in domestic payment transactions;
              - coordinated cooperation of the Central Bank with Banking Agencies, in charge of
                bank licensing and supervision, as well as establishing standards for bank
                operations, according to the practice in the developed market countries;
              - The payment system implemented - internal payment transactions transferred to
                commercial banks. The commercial banks have taken over the payment
                transactions on 5 January 2001. The Central Bank carries out the settlement of
                internal payment transactions through RTGS system and gyro clearing. The project
                was assisted by international community. The financial assistance was particularly
                provided by the USAID;
              - The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina provided banking operations for the
                Government of Bosnia and Herzegovina and for some other customers;
              - It maintains the adequate co-operation concerning the issues within the work
                scope covered by the Central Bank, with the institutions at the state level -
                the BiH Presidency, the BiH Council of Ministers and the BiH Parliament as well as
                the Entity institutions - especially with Ministries of Finance, banking agencies and
                deposit insurance agencies and relevant international financial institutions,
                particularly International Monetary Fund.

           Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina has performed its objectives and tasks defined
           by Law and the Strategic aims and it successfully operated, confirming its role and affir-
           mation in Bosnia and Herzegovina and wider.

Payments System

CBBH took a leading role in establishment of the new payments system in BiH. We estab-
lished RTGS clearing system (real time gross settlements system) for the settlement of
transactions of big values and gyro clearing system for the large volume of transactions
of small value. Central Bank of BH runs the Gyro Clearing system through centers locat-
ed in the CBBH Main Office, Main Units in Mostar and Sarajevo and Main Bank of
Republika Srpska of CBBH in Banja Luka.

New payments systems (RTGS and Gyro Clearing) proved to be very efficient and cheap.
During 2001, both systems operated smoothly. We introduced a third daily settlement for
gyro clearing system starting from 3 December 2001. We contracted jobs related to imple-
mentation of the dislocated back up system. The existing systems are improved, new pro-
grams are installed, such as "End of day Netting" (the transfer of funds from the transaction
account to commercial bank's reserve account at the end of the working day) and the acqui-
sition of 'Firewall' started. We established the co-operation with the Central Register for the
securities and we had negotiations with companies VISA NNSS -National Net Settlement
Service, Europay and local firms and we considered the possibilities for the CBBH to be the
agent in settlement in the card operations.

Banking Sector

In 2001, the CBBH took over the activities in the coordination of the work with the bank-
ing agencies, follow up of the situation in the banking sector and it had the coordinat-
ing role in the activities of the banking supervision.

The coordinating role of the Central Bank of BH with the Banking supervision agencies
was directed towards the activities on Agencies operations, issuing of the banking licens-
es, compatibility of the legal regulations, following of the situation and restructuring of
the total banking system, banks privatization, trends in financial institutions of BH and
activities and the central Bank issues and issues of the banking agencies in new payments
system.

The III International conference on banking sector was held, which addressed certain
issues in order to further improve the overall banking sector. It was stated at the confer-
ence that the most successful reforms in BiH have been achieved in monetary and bank-
ing sector, but that the successful transformation in this area is not followed by changes
in other sectors. Special importance for the banking sector development has been the
arrival of the foreign banks in BH. The announcement of the arrival of foreign banks is a
good sign for strengthening of the banking sector in BH. In this respect, the continuance
of the privatization process and the merging of banks, as well as strengthening of the
banks supervision and introduction of the agencies for the banking supervision under the
CBBH umbrella are also important.

The main changes that have brought the improvement in the BiH banking sector about
include:
    - Payments system reform,
    - Increases in minimum capital requirements,
    - Legal changes that made it possible for a bank registered in one entity to operate
      branches in the other entity,
    - A number of foreign banks commenced operations in BiH in the last two years.
      Increased foreign competition has already been very good for the BiH economy.

While the primary responsibility in the banking supervision area lies with the Entity Banking

foreign assets. The same ratio is in the liability structure, 75% local currency liabilities and

Euro Changeover

The Euro changeover went very smoothly in BiH despite the very large quantities of cur-
rency that have been handled by the commercial banks and the CBBH. Since 1998, when
the first KM banknotes were put into circulation, a total of around DM 6 billion DM ban-
knotes and coins have been sent back to Germany from BiH.from the banks. During 2001
and the first two months of 2002, almost DM 4 billion of DM banknotes were converted
in BiH. Most of tis occurred in the five months from October 2001 to February 2002.They
were converted in approximately equal proportions into KM currency, bank deposits in
BiH banks and into Euro currency.

As a consequence, in the last three months of 2001, KM in circulation rose by KM 920
million or 104%. Coincidently, citizens bank deposits in BiH also grew by 104 % in the
last three months of 2001. This is a clear sign that citizens have started to trust the bank-
ing system with their savings. The large amounts of DM converted in BiH and the large
proportion of them that have been converted to KM currency or put on deposit in a BiH
bank led to an unprecedented growth in the country's official foreign exchange reserves.
These reserves were KM 1,235 million at 30 September 2001. By 31 December 2001
they were KM 2,697 million, a growth of 118% in three months. The foreign reserves
now represent around five months of import cover.

2. Required reserves

The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina performs the settlement of required reserves
in accordance with the CBBH Law and the decision, which regulated that required
reserves should be maintained by holding cash or money deposits with the Central Bank
through its head office or main units and should be processed as average daily reserves
in a ten-day period.

In 2001, the average funds for required reserve maintenance (balance of banks' reserve
accounts and cash balance) amounted to KM 340 million, which is higher by 46,4% com-
pared to the total funds for the required reserve maintenance in 2000. The increase of the
total funds for maintaining required reserves was influenced by the increase of cash in the
vaults with commercial banks, which in 2001 recorded a 253,7% increase. In 2001, bank
cash took 28,8% of the total funds for required reserve maintenance (it took 11,9% in
2000).

equivalent of five percents of the total amount of their particular liabilities.

more non-consecutive periods. There was no need to initiate the procedure of closing

4. Retail prices, inflation and costs of living

In the Federation of BiH, retail prices increased by 1,7% in 2001 compared to the aver-
age retail prices in 2000. During the first nine months, retail prices had a slow downward
trend, and started to increase in September. The structure of retail prices recorded
increase of prices of all products and services, except for the prices of agricultural prod-
ucts, which decreased by 1,6% compared to 2000.

The retail prices in Republika Srpska recorded an increase by 6,5% in 2001 compared to
2000. The highest increase was recorded in June and October. The structure of retail
prices recorded the highest increase with the prices of services (18,2%) and agricultural
products (12,7%). During the year, the biggest oscillations were recorded with prices of
agricultural products, which had an upward tendency in the first half of the year, and they
had a downward tendency in the second half of the year.

Inflation, measured by retail price growth rate over the current year compared to
December of the previous year, had different trends in the entities until December, when
in both entities it recorded increase.

Inflation in the Federation of BiH had a slow upward trend in the first quarter of 2001. It
was negative from March until December, when it amounted to 0,3%. In Republika
Srpska, inflation was negative in the first five months, and had upward tendency from
June to amount to 2,2,% in December.

 In the Federation of BiH, the total living costs in 2001 were higher by 2,1% compared
to the average in 2000, resulting from the increased costs of all products and services,
except for hygiene and health care costs.

The total costs of living in Republika Srpska during 2001 increased by 5,9% compared to
the average living costs in 2000, which was mostly the result of the increased traffic and
communications costs.


5. Employment

The number of employees in the Federation of BiH decreased during 2001 by 1,4%. So,
there were 405,689 employed persons in the end of the year. The decrease in the num-
ber of employees resulted from excluding the employees in Brcko District and solving
the status of a part of employees on stand-by.

In 2001, the number of the unemployed increased by 2,8%, so 269,004 unemployed
persons were registered with the Employment Bureau of FBiH in the end of December.
With respect to the qualification structure, the largest number of persons looking for a job
were unskilled workers (35,9%) and skilled workers (34,6%). The unemployment rate was
39,9% in December.

In the end of the year, 192,727 persons were employed in Republika Srpska (data from
September 2001).

In 2001, the number of the unemployed decreased by 3,6%, so 147,749 persons look-
ing for a job were registered with the Employment Bureau of Republika Srpska in the end
of December. The decrease in the number of the unemployed resulted from erasing from
records some persons due to their unregistered employment and due to other reasons.
The highest number of persons looking for a job accounts for skilled workers (37,7%) and
unskilled workers (29,5%). Unemployment rate in December was 40,0%.


6. Net wages

In 2001, the average net wages in both entities are presented according to the new
methodology, based on the Classification of Activities.

The average net wage in the Federation of BiH increased in 2001, so it amounted to KM
458 in December. This was the increase of 10,9% compared to the average net wage paid
in 2000, and the increase of 7,8% compared to that in December 2000.

                                 VI CONCLUSIONS



D  uring 2001, the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina operated with great suc-
   cess, as it is fully confirmed by the following results:

  1. The currency board principle has been strictly applied. The stability of the local
     currency has been maintained continuously. The balance of net foreign assets
     exceeds its monetary liabilities in Konvertible Marks by KM 75 million.

  2. Investment of foreign exchange reserves was performed under strict control and
     with constant monitoring, analyses and the obeying of the defined guidelines.
     Interest rates at the world money market, trends of exchange rates and ratings of
     correspondent banks with which the funds had been invested were constantly
     observed. On 31 December 2001, foreign exchange reserves of the Central Bank
     of Bosnia and Herzegovina amounted to KM 2,697 million. In the end of
     September, foreign exchange reserves amounted to around KM 1,235 million,
     which means that the foreign exchange reserves increased by KM 1,462 million in
     the last quarter. These foreign exchange reserves had already existed in BiH, but
     they were not in the banking system..

  3. The payment system reform was successfully implemented in 2001. There are 42
     banks in the payment system currently. The total number of transactions performed
     in the period from 05 January to 31 December 2001 was 11,916,222 million of
     transactions, and their total value amounted to KM 16,845 million. Settlement is
     carried out three times a day.

  4. The inclusion of Bosnia and Herzegovina into the international financial statistics is
     a positive success, which resulted from the cooperation of BiH commercial banks,
     International Monetary Fund and the Central Bank of BiH.

Form of presentation of financial statements

The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina financial statements have been prepared in
accordance with the Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and
Herzegovina and the Law on Internal Payment Operations, and in accordance with
International Accounting Standards.

The Financial Statements have been prepared in accordance with the historical cost basis
of accounting modified for revaluation as disclosed below. A summary of the principal
accounting policies is set out below.


Reporting Currency

The accompanying Financial Statements are reported in the official national currency of
Bosnia and Herzegovina - Convertible Marka (KM).

The official exchange rate to German Mark (DEM) has been defined by the Law of Bosnia
and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina as 1 KM :1 DEM. The
Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina is required by the Law to purchase and sell with-
out restrictions KM on demand for DEM within the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina
at the exchange rate defined as one to one.

As of 1 January 2002 the EUR was introduced, and the Central Bank of Bosnia and
Herzegovina has defined fixed rate between 1 KM :1 EUR as 1.95583 KM = 1 EUR.


Currency Board Rule

The Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina
requires that the aggregate amount of its monetary liabilities shall at no time exceed the
equivalent of its net foreign exchange reserves. Details of compliance with the rule are

Grants

Grants related to assets, including non-monetary grants, are recorded at nominal value
by setting up the grant as deferred income, which is recognized as income proportion-
ately to expenses already incurred (including depreciation over useful life of the asset).


Number of employees

As per 31 December 2001, the Bank had 241 employees (31 December 2000: 194
employees).


Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are recorded at historical cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation
is calculated using the straight-line method. The following depreciation rates have been
applied:
                  Computer equipment                       20,0%

                  Transport vehicles                       15,5%
                  Switchboard                              20,0%
                  Buildings                                1,3%
                  Other buildings                          3,5%

Interest Income and Expense Recognition

Interest income and expenses are recognized on an accrual basis.


Taxes

The Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina
exempts the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina, its assets, property and income,
and its operations and transactions, from all taxes and duties. Payroll taxes and other
taxes on salaries are not covered by this exemption.


Foreign Currency Translation

Transactions denominated in foreign currencies have been translated to KM at exchange
rates applying on the trade date of transactions. Assets and liabilities denominated in for-
eign currencies are translated into KM at foreign exchange rates applying at the balance
sheet date. Gains and losses on translation are credited or charged to the profit and loss
account.

33) Segment Reporting

The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina operates as a central bank under a currency
board arrangement. Its domestic operations are in Bosnia and Herzegovina and foreign
investment activities are predominantly carried out in Europe.


34) Performance of clearing and distribution of Convertible Marka

Until December 31, 2000 the Payment Bureaus performed KM clearing for local com-
mercial banks with settlement of these clearings occurring through reserve accounts
maintained by commercial banks at the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Until
December 31, 2000 the Payment Bureaus also distributed KM currency, throughout the
country acting as an agent of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Since January 5, 2001 the KM clearing for local commercial banks has been carried out
through the RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) and (Bulk) Gyro Clearing systems within
the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina; through reserve accounts of commercial
banks opened with the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Also, since January 5,
2001 distribution of KM for the whole country has been done through separate vaults of
the organisational units and branches of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Domestic payments are executed by the licensed commercial banks, participants of the
interbank clearing system.
