                                                                                                  Summary
           Summary by the Governor of the Central Bank of BiH



T   he Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina is a State institution charged with the inde-
    pendent implementation of monetary policy. The implementation of monetary policy
in BiH is conducted by a "Currency Board" arrangement. The local currency, the
Convertible Mark is tied one to one to the Deutchemark in the CBBH Law, and is now tied
to the Euro at the same rate through the Deutschemark (1 KM = 0.511 Euro). The KM lia-
bilities of CBBH also have to be fully backed with convertible foreign assets. This is the
essence of the currency board arrangement.

During 2000 the Currency Board requirements of full convertibility and full coverage for
the KM were met at all times. As a consequence of the soundness of the currency board
arrangement, confidence in the stability of the KM remained high during 2000, inflation
remained at a reasonable level, and the use of the KM continued to expand. The growth
in the CBBH`s foreign reserves in 2000 was not as spectacular as in 1999, but foreign
reserves still rose during 2000 from DM 865 million at the beginning of the year to DM
1,044 million by the end of the year. When the CBBH`s foreign reserves reached the mile-
stone of DM 1 billion late in December 2000, this meant that the foreign reserves of the
CBBH had grown by more than 750% since the CBBH was established in August 1997.

The CBBH balance sheet continued to gain in straight in 2000 as the "free foreign
exchange reserves" of the CBBH (i.e. those that were above level needed to cover all the
KM liabilities) increased form DM 29 million at the beginning of the 2000 to DM 48 mil-
lion at the end of 2000. The sources of the CBBH "free reserves" are the initial capital injec-
tion of KM 25 million the CBBH received and operational profits the CBBH has generat-
ed over the last two years. The CBBH Law requires the CBBH to build its capital to a min-
imum level of 5% of its KM liabilities. This level was reached for the first time at the end
of 2000. Once this first " target" for capital is reached, the CBBH Law requires the CBBH
to than build its reserves up to 5% of its KM liabilities so that capital and reserves in
aggregate equal at least 10% of the CBBH`s KM liabilities. This statutory requirement to
strengthen the balance sheet of the CBBH is very important for the maintenance and sus-
tainability of the currency board arrangement.

The continued growth in the KM as the transactions currency in BiH and as the currency
in which a growing proportion of bank deposits are denominated show that citizens of
BiH understand and trust the currency board and the KM. This trust is very important and
the CBBH will do everything to continue to justify that trust.

One other major task of CBBH in 2000 was the implementation of the new domestic non-
cash payments system in BiH. While many other institutions and people were also
involved, the CBBH key role as the builder and operator of the new Clearing Houses and
as the Chair of the Payments Council of BiH. The new payments system took effect on 5th
January 2001, but almost all of the work needed to introduce the system was done in
2000. The CBBH has introduced two Clearing Houses, a real time RTGS system that is
based in CBBH`s Head Office, and a giro clearing house that has clearings centers in the
CBBH’s Main Unites in Banja Luka, Mostar and Sarajevo. The new clearing houses are sim-
ilar to system being used in a number of other European countries. But they were put in
place in BiH in a faster time than had been achieved in any of the other countries where
they had been introduced. This was a credit to all who were involved and showed it is
possible to implement complex projects in BiH quickly and effectively.

For the new payments system to work, the commercial banks also had to be ready to take
over payments operations from the state-owned payments bureaus. It was a clear sign of
the continuing improvement of the banking system in BiH that 40 commercial banks were
ready to participate when it commenced on 5th January 2001.

There were other important steps during 2000 that will encourage the development of a
sounder, stronger banking sector in BiH; one that can play a more significant role in sav-
ings and lending and thus in economic development in BiH. The minimum capital
required by a bank is being progressively increased, it became possible for a bank to
operate branches in both entities, deposit insurance was introduced in the Federation, a
number of foreign banks entered the market and a considerable amount of consolidation
occurred through mergers or closures.

There is still a long way to go before BiH has a banking system that can play the major
economic role that the banking system plays in most other countries in Europe. But 2000
will go down as the year in which the banking sector finally began to move in this direc-
tion and it is likely that the speed of change will increase in 2001 and beyond.

During 2000, there were two more important developments relating to the KM curren-
cy. Firstly, 1 KM and 2 KM coins were introduced in July 2000. This brought the number
of KM coins to five. There has been a marked shift to the KM as the transactions curren-
cy in all of BiH over the last two years.

The second important development was the approval by the Presidency of BiH for the
introduction of a KM 200 banknote. The significant element of this decision was that the
Presidency agreed that there should be only one version of this banknote. The banknote
will be put into circulation later in 2001.

During 2000, the CBBH opened an office in Brcko, the CBBH`s fifth office. Staff numbers
grew by 41 during 2000, largely because of an increase in the number of banking cus-
tomers of the CBBH, a decision to increase security within our offices, the CBBH`s new
role in the domestic payment system and the opening of the Brcko office. By the end of
2000, the CBBH had 198 employees. I wish to thank the CBBH Governing Board, Senior
Management, and all staff for their contributions to the CBBH`s operations and continued
development during 2000.

                                                                                                   Introduction
                                        INTRODUCTION


T  he Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina (hereinafter referred to as: the Central
   Bank) was established in accordance with the Law adopted at the Parliament of Bosnia
and Herzegovina on 20 June 1997. The Central Bank started its operation on 11 August
1997. The main objectives and basic tasks of the Central Bank, defined by the Law and in
accordance with the General Peace Agreement in Bosnia and Herzegovina are:

       - to achieve and maintain monetary stability by issuing domestic currency according
         to the arrangement known as a Currency Board, i.e. with full coverage in freely con-
         vertible foreign exchange funds, under fixed exchange rate: one Convertible Mark
         per one Deutsche Mark;
·      - to define and control the implementation of monetary policy of Bosnia and
         Herzegovina;
       - to hold and manage, in a safe and profitable way, the official foreign exchange
         reserves, acquired by issuing domestic currency;
       - to support or to establish and maintain appropriate payment and settlement sys
         tems;
       - to co-ordinate the activities of the Entity Banking Agencies which are in charge of
         bank licensing and supervision;
       - to receive deposits from institutions at the level of Bosnia and Herzegovina,
         deposits from Entities and their public institutions according to the joint decision of
         Entities, as well as deposits from commercial banks;
       - to issue rules and guidelines for the implementation of the activities of the Central
         Bank, within its powers as defined by the Law;
       - to take part in international organisations engaged in strengthening financial and
         economic stability, and to represent Bosnia and Herzegovina in intergovernmental
         organisations regarding issues of monetary policy.

The senior body of the Central Bank is the Governing Board, which is in charge of estab-
lishing and supervision of monetary policy, organisation and strategies of the Central
Bank, all according to powers given to the Board by the Law. The Governing Board con-
sists of the Governor, who is Chairman of the Governing Board, and three members (two
from the Federation of BiH - Bosniak and Croat and one from Republika Srpska - Serb).
The Governor is a foreign national, Peter Nicholl. The Governor is also the chief execu-
tive in charge of daily operation of the CBBH. There is also the executive Management of
the Central Bank consisting of the Governor and three Vice Governors (appointed by the
Governor, with approval of the Governing Board). The Management is responsible for the
operational management of the CBBH. The CBBH employees are organised in three
departments which Vice Governors are in charge of.

    The Central Bank operates through its Head Office and three Main Units (in Sarajevo,
    Mostar and Banja Luka, with Main Unit Banja Luka having a Branch at Pale). In 2000, a
    Branch of the Central Bank in Brcko was established.

    This report gives the review of activities and results of the CBBH work in 2000.

Payments Reform

In 1999, the Council of Ministers of BiH accepted a strategy proposed by the international
community for a fundamental reform of the BiH domestic payments system. The core of
the strategy was to shift the domestic payments from the Payments Bureaus, that had a
monopoly on such transactions, to the commercial banks and, once that was done, to
close the Payments Bureaus.

The Governing Board of the CBBH decided in February 2000 that the CBBH should take
the lead in designing and establishing the new clearing systems that would be used in the
reformed payments system in BiH. This decision was endorsed by the National Payments
Council of BiH.

The CBBH received funding from USAID to fund the development of the new clearing
houses. It was decided to build two clearing houses: an RTGS system (Real Time Gross
Settlement System) through which large value transactions would be settled and a bulk
giro system for the large volume of small transactions. The giro system is run through
three clearing centers located in the CBBH Main Units in Banja Luka, Mostar and Sarajevo.

The two systems were tendered separately but both tenders were won by the firm Logica
from London. In the case of the giro clearing house, the tender was a joint one between
Logica and Halcom from Slovenia. There was a very tight timetable if the deadline set by
the international community and accepted by the Council of Ministers of BiH of the end
of 2000 was to be met. But thanks to the hard work and good cooperation of the suppli-
ers, the commercial banks, the Ministries of Finance, the Banking Agencies, and CBBH,
the new payments system was able to start operation on 5 January 2001. The new clear-
ing houses in BiH are similar to those in operation in many other European countries, so
that BiH can now claim to have a modern payments system. The implementation of the
clearing house project was done in a faster time than had been achieved in any of the
other countries. This was a credit to all who were involved and showed it is possible to
implement complex project quickly and effectively in BiH.


Banking Sector

An efficient, effective and safe banking system is a crucial part of a market economy. First, it
encourages people to save by providing them with good savings instruments. Second, it
promotes investment and economic growth by channeling those savings to people and busi-
nesses who wish to borrow to finance the establishment or expansion of their businesses.

But BiH does not yet have an efficient or effective banking system. The banking system in
BiH is still relatively weak and fragmented and plays only a limited role in the economy
as the level of savings people hold in banks is low and amount of lending the banks do
to finance the business or the household sectors is also low.

In 2000, this gloomy picture began to change. First, as discussed in the previous section,
the payment system was fundamentally reformed. Second, the RS government passed an
amendment to their banking law that opened up the possibility of a bank authorised in
one entity of BiH operating as a branch in the other entity. One bank form the Federation
got approval from the RS Banking Agency to open a branch in the RS in late 2000 and
other banks form both entities followed in early 2001.

Third, most of the state-owned banks finally had their opening balance sheets agreed and
they moved into the privatisation phase. But progress in bank privatisation remained very
slow in 2000.

Fourth, a Deposit Insurance Agency was formed in the Federation of BiH, and a Deposit
Insurance Agency in RS is to commence its operations soon.

Fifth, more foreign banks commenced operations in BiH, either by applying for licenses
directly or by purchasing a share-holding in an existing BiH bank. Increased foreign com-
petition will be very good for BiH economy. It will give depositors and borrowers more
choice and will force the local banks to improve the range and the quality of the banking
services they provide.

There is still a long way to go before BiH has a banking sector that can play the major eco-
nomic role that the banking sector plays in most other countries in Europe. But 2000 will
be seen as the year in which the banking sector finally began to move in this direction and
it is certain that the speed of change will accelerate even more in 2001.

                                        II CONTROL FUNCTION



          T  he Central Bank has established the regular control function with the purpose of carry-
             ing out the control of operational risk, as well as correct application of all general reg-
          ulations concerning the CBBH operation.

          During 2000, the control was focussed on the review of the CBBH official foreign reserves,
          balance of the CBBH monetary liabilities, particularly KM bank notes, the control of imple-
          mentation of the Currency Board principle, control of the CBBH income and expenditures,

          Heads of organisational units) written reports on reviews and controls, including findings,

                                                                                                 Organisation
                 III ORGANISATION OF THE CENTRAL BANK



O    rganisational structure of the Central Bank is established in order to meet the needs
     for the successful carrying out of all its activities as set under the Law on the CBBH.

The chief body of the Central Bank is the Governing Board consisting of the Governor and
three members - two from the Federation of BiH and one from Republika Srpska. During
2000, at the meetings of the Governing Board taking place as needed and not less than
once monthly, the Governing Board discussed all important issues related to the realisa-
tion of basic tasks and objectives of the Central Bank.

While the strategic direction of the CBBH is set by the Governing Board, the
Management, consisting of the Governor as the chief executive and three Vice Governors,
implements the strategies. The Management is responsible for operational management
of everyday operations and realisation of tasks and objectives set by the Law on the CBBH
and Strategic Plan, as well as by the Decisions of the Governing Board. During 2000, the
Governing Board met 20 times, and the Management team meets twice a week.

In organisational structure, all the activities of the Central Bank are classified in terms of
the nature of the tasks and duties: three departments are in the CBBH headed by the Vice
Governors. The Central Bank has three Main Units - in Sarajevo, Mostar, and Banja Luka,
with Main Unit Banja Luka having a Branch Office in Pale. In 2000, the Central Bank
opened a Branch Office seated in Brcko.

The Central Bank of BiH adapted its organisational structure to the introduction of a new
payment system in Bosnia and Herzegovina by establishing Directorate for Payment
Systems and the Section for Payment Transactions of the Central Bank of BiH.

During 2000, 41 new person was employed so that the Central Bank currently has 198
employees. During work in the Central Bank, the staff undergoes regular training in order
to provide successful work.

2. Industrial Production

Industrial production in the Federation of BiH in 2000 is monitored according to a new
methodology, so that classification of industrial products upon their purposes is changed.
Compared to the average of 1999, the total of industrial production increased by 8,8%,
which was mainly the result of the increase in energy production which takes 35,34% of
the total industrial production. The industrial production achieved its biggest volume in
the fourth quarter, when it increased by 17,8 - 21,7%.

Industrial production in Republika Srpska in 2000 is higher by 5,6% compared to the pre-
vious year. During the previous year, the biggest increase was achieved by the produc-
tion of non-ferrous metals and chemical base industry.

Given the purpose of industrial production, the production of raw materials increased by
8,6% during 2000, the production of instruments of labour increased by 2,3%, and pro-
duction of consumer goods decreased by 1,5% when compared to the average in 1999.

4. Retail prices, inflation and costs of living

Retail prices in Bosnia and Herzegovina in 2000, in comparison to the average in 1999,
recorded increasing trends in both Entities. However, the prices in the Federation of BiH
recorded a slower increasing trend than those in Republika Srpska.

In the Federation of BiH retail prices were marked with a moderate growth, so during the
year they increased by 1,2% on the average compared to the previous year. The structure
of retail prices recorded the biggest oscillations in the prices of agricultural products- after
growing in the first five months, they started to fall, so that in the end of the year their
increase amounted to only 0,5%.

The retail prices in Republika Srpska in 2000 recorded an increase by 13,6% compared to
average prices in 1999, which resulted from the increase of prices of agricultural products
and prices of services. The biggest rise of prices was recorded in the fourth quarter.



                                                        - 27 -
 Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina




The import of goods in Entities was oriented at different markets (Republika Srpska was
oriented to Yugoslavia, and the Federation of BiH to Croatia). Therefore, the prices were
different by Entities. The prices in Republika Srpska were lower since this market was sup-
plied from Yugoslavia. As BiH market is now unique and integrated, it is being liberated
and prices are being levelled. A more open market between the Entities brought about
the increase of prices in Republika Srpska, and their decrease in the Federation of BiH.

Inflation in Bosnia and Herzegovina, measured by retail price growth rate over the cur-
rent year compared to December of the previous year, had increasing trends in both enti-
ties. However, the increasing trends did not record the same intensity.

At the end of 2000, inflation rate in the Federation of BiH was 4,0%, and in Republika
Srpska it was 16,1%, as distinguished from the previous year when the inflation rate in
Federation of BiH was negative amounting to -1,0% and in Republika Srpska it was 14,0%.
Inflation, measured by average retail prices in 2000 compared to 1999, was 1,2% in the
Federation of BiH and it was 13,6% in Republika Srpska.

In Federation of BiH, the total living costs were higher by 1,4% compared to the average
in 1999, resulting from the increased costs of transport and communications and
decreased food costs.

The total costs of living in Republika Srpska during 2000 increased by 13,4% compared
to the previous year, which was mostly affected by the increased costs of transport and
communications and costs of education and culture. The highest increase of all kinds of
costs was recorded in the fourth quarter.

                                 VI CONCLUSIONS



D   uring 2000, the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina operated with great success,
    as it is fully confirmed by the following summarised results:

  1. There has been continuous maintenance of stability of domestic currency, which is
     fully confirmed by liquid money supply in domestic currency fully covered by the
     foreign assets (official foreign reserves managed by the CBBiH). Thus, there is the
     strict implementation of the Currency Board arrangement.
     Net foreign assets are higher than the monetary liabilities by KM 48 million.

  2. Non-cash payment transactions were carried out only in domestic currency. This
     resulted in the increase of total foreign reserves held with the CBBH and commer-
     cial banks.

  3. Investment of foreign reserves was performed under strict control and with con-
     stant monitoring and analyses in order to insure the necessary liquidity and the
     obeying of the defined guidelines. Interest rates at the world money market, trends
     of exchange rates and ratings of correspondent banks with which the funds had
     been invested, as well as those of other banks with the purpose of finding those
     banks which offer the most favourable investment conditions, were constantly
     observed.

  4. The activities of preparing domestic payment system reform were successfully com-
     pleted in 2000. In the beginning of January 2001, commercial banks took over pay-
     ment operations. All the transactions in gyro and RTGS systems are being per-
     formed through the Central Bank.

Form of presentation of financial statements

The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina financial statements have been prepared in
accordance with the Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and
Herzegovina and the Law on Internal Payment Operations. International Accounting
Standards have been used.

The Financial Statements have been prepared in accordance with the historical cost basis
of accounting modified for revaluation as disclosed below. A summary of the principal
accounting policies is set out below.


Reporting Currency

The accompanying Financial Statements are reported in the official national currency of
Bosnia and Herzegovina - Convertible Marka (KM).

The official exchange rate to German Mark (DEM) has been defined by the Law of Bosnia
and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina as 1 KM :1 DEM. The
Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina is required by the Law to purchase and sell with-
out restrictions KM on demand for DEM within the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina for
the exchange rate defined as one to one.


Currency Board Rule

The Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina
requires that the aggregate amount of its monetary liabilities shall at no time exceed the
equivalent of its net foreign exchange reserves.


Grants

Grants related to assets, including non-monetary grants, are recorded at nominal value
by setting up the grant as deferred income, which is recognized as income proportion-
ately to expenses already incurred (including depreciation over useful life of the asset).


Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are recorded at historical cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation is cal-
culated using the straight-line method. The following depreciation rates have been applied:
                   Computer equipment                  20.0%
                   Software                            20.0%
                   Transport vehicle                   15.5%
                   Switchboard                         20.0%
                   Buildings                            1.3%
                   Other fixed assets                  10.0% - 16.5%
                                             - 37-
 Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina




Interest Income and Expense Recognition

Interest income and expenses are recognized on an accrual basis.


Taxes

The Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina
exempts the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina, its assets, property and income,
and its operations and transactions, from all taxes and duties. Payroll taxes and other
taxes on salaries are not covered by this exemption.


Foreign Currency Translation

Assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies other than DEM are translated
into KM at foreign exchange rates at the year end. Gains and losses on translation are
credited or charged to the profit and loss statement.


Payment Bureau

The Payment Bureaus for Bosnia Herzegovina (the Payment Bureaus) performed KM
clearing for local commercial banks with settlement of these clearings occurring through
reserve accounts maintained by commercial banks at the Central Bank of Bosnia and
Herzegovina. The Payment Bureaus also distributed KM currency, throughout the coun-
try acting as an agent of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.


Number of employees

As per 31 December 2000, the Bank had 194 employees (31 December 1999: 157
employees).

33) Related Party and other Disclosures

There were no related-party transactions with members of the Governing Board. Loans to
staff are made in accordance with the provisions of Law of Bosnia and Herzegovina on
the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The Governor of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina is also a member of the
Payment Bureaus Governing Board and Payment Bureaus acted as an agency for KM cur-
rency distribution in the country.


34) Segment Reporting

The Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina operates as a central bank under a currency
board arrangement. Its domestic operations are in one geographical area (Bosnia and
Herzegovina) and foreign investment activities are predominantly carried out in Europe.


35) Subsequent events

Until December 31, 2000 the Payment Bureaus performed KM clearing for local com-
mercial banks with settlement of these clearings occurring through reserve accounts
maintained by commercial banks at the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Until
December 31, 2000 the Payment Bureaus also distributed KM currency, throughout the
country acting as an agent of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Since January 5, 2001 the KM clearing for local commercial banks has been carried out
through the RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) and (Bulk) Gyro Clearing systems within
the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina; through reserve accounts of commercial
banks opened with the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Also, since January 5,
2001 distribution of KM for the whole country has been done through separate vaults of
the Main Units of the Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
